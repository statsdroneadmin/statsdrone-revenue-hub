# John Wright — Speaking & Writing Style Guide

For drafting posts, show notes, emails, and social in John's voice for **Revenue Optimization with StatsDrone** (formerly Affiliate BI). Grounded in solo and guest transcripts. Prefer real patterns over "authentic" filler.

---

## 1. Voice (one paragraph)

John sounds like an operator thinking out loud on a walk: practical, opinionated, a little restless. He sells through pain and proof—revenue leak, shaving, closed casinos, broken APIs—not through BI jargon. He admits uncertainty ("I have no conclusive proof," "this is not fully conclusive"), then still takes a stand ("my strong opinion," "I'm here to say"). He riffs with "yeah," "look," "I mean," "kind of," "wait a minute," and "you know what," stacks short clauses into longer runs, and often lands on a shipping takeaway: measure player value, own your data, focus on one thing, or stop pretending in-house tracking is easy. Humor is dry and self-implicating (failed builds, LinkedIn polls, Salesforce stock as a side note—not investment advice). He talks to affiliates, operators, and affiliate programs as peers who can act this week.

---

## 2. Do / Don't

**Do**
- Lead with a concrete problem: clicks not registering, default campaigns with no recourse, withdrawal caps as a funding signal, Tableau licenses nobody needs anymore.
- Name the show correctly: *Revenue Optimization with StatsDrone* (older eps: Affiliate BI). Sponsor line can include **StatsDrone** and **Affiliate Track**; other real partners from eps when relevant (e.g. IGsuite, Affilka)—never invent sponsors.
- Use operator / affiliate / affiliate program language. Prefer "revenue leak" and "player value" over abstract "insights."
- Soft-sell: "I'd love to have more customers give us a try," "join us in the discussion on LinkedIn," "usually wearing purple" at conferences—not hard closes.
- Share first-person receipts: LinkedIn polls, Sigma World talks, coffee with someone in Montreal, rebuilding after shutting the first stats app down.
- Flip the frame mid-thought: "to kind of flip the narrative…," "let's flip this around…," "let's start with the elephant in the room…"

**Don't**
- Sound like a BI brochure ("unlock synergistic analytics"). John explicitly says selling "BI as BI" doesn't land—pain does.
- Invent product names, competitors, or conference claims. Stick to brands he actually uses (see §6).
- Over-polish: clean light ASR mess, but keep spoken rhythm. Don't turn him into LinkedIn-guru cadence.
- Pretend every claim is settled. He hedges on shaving signatures and incomplete datasets, then still shares the useful signal.
- Roast guests or competitors with snark. Competitors get named as market context (Nifty Stats, WeCanTrack, etc.); tone stays "rising tides lift all boats."
- Write guest answers in his voice. Guest eps: only host questions, bridges, and short personal asides.

## 3. Sentence rhythm and filler habits

- **Openers:** "Welcome to…," "Today I wanna talk about…," "So let me just rewind…," "Okay, so I think I kind of see where the story is going…"
- **Fillers / discourse markers (use sparingly in writing, freely in spoken-style copy):** yeah · look · I mean · kind of / kinda · wait a minute · you know what · so · like · it's like · going,
- **Pattern:** long run-on thought → short punch → soft close.  
  Example shape: *On paper it's true… until you start testing these APIs and go, wait a minute, this data isn't lining up… Yeah, focus on one thing, do it well.*
- **Emphasis moves:** "I'm here to say…," "my strong opinion…," "it goes without saying…," "simply put…," "the signature here is…"
- **Closers:** "So I hope that's helpful…," "There you have it…," "Let me know what you think…," rating/review ask on podcast outros.

## 4. How he asks questions vs how he riffs solo

**Solo / John-heavy**
- Mini-essays: problem → story or data → opinion → implication for affiliates/operators → soft CTA.
- Often sparked by a LinkedIn poll, a conference Q, a coffee chat, or something he just built (Claude, Lovable, vibe-coded CRM).
- He timestamps the year ("fun 2026," "putting my timestamp on this year") and compares old tools (Tableau, Salesforce, pixel tracking) to the new stack (Claude, on-prem, vibe coding).

**Guest interviews**
- Warm, stacked questions: "Would you say that…," "But is that actually…," "Okay. And let's focus on the opposite…," "So let's flip this around…"
- Bridges with agreement then a pivot: "Yeah. That kind of leads to…," "Okay, that's amazing. Because…"
- Short personal dump (we add ~50 programs/month and close ~30; we shut down our first app) then hands the mic back.
- Ends with: thank guest → "how can people get a hold of you?" → optional program plug → show outro.

## 5. Recurring topics and framing

| Theme | How he frames it |
| --- | --- |
| **Data / BI** | Collect → process → automate insights. Sell the *pain* (revenue leak), not the category name. |
| **Shaving / trust** | Uncomfortable but important. Dynamic variables + unique click IDs = recourse when operators inject winners into default campaigns. |
| **First-party** | Newsletters, apps, audiences—affiliates optimizing what they have as SEO gets harder. |
| **On-prem / data ownership** | Affiliates fear SaaS peering at their numbers; on-prem / self-host (e.g. "dido") is the trust answer. Same logic for CRM/chat: don't leave crown jewels only in someone else's cloud. |
| **AI / vibe coding** | Real disruption. Claude/ChatGPT/Lovable for data viz and apps; Tableau/Power BI/Salesforce under pressure; security is the catch. Race: keep product so useful that DIY isn't worth the time. |
| **Operators vs affiliates** | Operators/programs: quality traffic, player value, brand launches, closed-program "sunken ships." Affiliates: negotiate deals, revenue architecture, stop building every tool in-house. |
| **In-house tracking** | Looks easy (there's an API); is brutal (mismatches, scrapers, years of edge cases). Focus on marketing/SEO; let specialists do stats. |
| **Market awareness** | Stats trackers are under-searched; rising tide with rivals; target "less than 1% → 5–10%." |

## 8. Samples (labeled — write fresh copy in this voice; not verbatim transcripts)

### Sample LinkedIn post (~150 words)

**SAMPLE — LinkedIn**

Affiliates still tell me they're going to build an in-house stats tracker. On paper it sounds easy: there's an API, pull the numbers, done.

Wait a minute. Then the login totals don't match. Scrapers break. One program returns clicks another way. Two years later you're maintaining glue code instead of growing traffic.

We lived that. We shipped a first version, shut it down, and came back because the hard part isn't the demo—it's covering hundreds of programs and keeping the data honest.

Same story with shaving: if everything lands in a default campaign, you have no recourse. Dynamic variables and unique click IDs change that conversation.

If you're optimizing revenue in iGaming or anywhere affiliates meet operators, stop treating visibility like a nice-to-have. Measure player value. Own your first-party story. And don't reinvent the wheel just because the spreadsheet feels safer.

Happy to talk shop on LinkedIn—or find us at the next show, usually in purple.

## Quick checklist before shipping copy

- [ ] Sounds spoken, not brochure-written  
- [ ] Pain or decision first; product second  
- [ ] StatsDrone / Affiliate Track / iGaming / operators / affiliate programs used correctly  
- [ ] No invented sponsors  
- [ ] At least one concrete example (API mismatch, click ID, withdrawal limit, LinkedIn poll, build story)  
- [ ] Soft CTA (discuss, try, see you at a conference), not hype
- [ ] No em dashes (—) in the shipped HTML

## 9. Punctuation for written posts (blog, LinkedIn, site copy)

**Do not use em dashes (—) in blog posts or polished site copy.** They read as default AI cadence.

Prefer:
- periods and new sentences
- commas
- parentheses for asides
- colons sparingly
- ellipses (…) only when mirroring spoken hesitation

The transcript samples above may still contain em dashes from speech cleanup. Do not copy that punctuation into articles.

Also avoid stacked "X — Y — Z" listicle headings. Use plain periods or "with" / commas in H2s.
